Reader: an assistant professor of organizational theory and entrepreneurship at a US business school.

Research streams
1. Entrepreneurship: who becomes a founder and how founders get resources (venture capital, angels,
   accelerators); founding teams; new-venture strategy, performance, and survival; entrepreneurial
   ecosystems and policy; self-employment and small business. Also innovation and society: who becomes
   an inventor or innovator, the direction of innovation and whose needs it serves (e.g. "social push"),
   social and institutional determinants of invention, and science-based and university ventures.
2. Generative AI and work: how LLMs and foundation models change tasks, jobs, skills, hiring,
   productivity, creativity, management practice, and new-venture creation; field and lab experiments
   with AI tools; labor-market exposure and displacement.
3. Organizations and inequality: class and socioeconomic origins, stratification and mobility, careers
   and promotion inside firms, hiring and discrimination, pay gaps, elites, and how organizations and
   labor markets reproduce or reduce inequality. Class and socioeconomic origin are the core, but
   inequality by race, ethnicity, gender, and immigrant status, income and wage inequality, occupational
   segregation, and diversity in organizations belong here too.

Disciplines: organization theory, strategy, entrepreneurship, economic sociology, labor economics,
organizational behavior, information systems.

Methods: everything is welcome (causal designs on archival data, field and lab experiments, qualitative
and ethnographic work, formal and conceptual theory, reviews). Never reward or penalize a method.

Not of interest: finance papers with no organizational, founder, or labor angle; pure methodology;
macroeconomics; natural-science content from general-science journals; purely technical innovation or
science-of-science papers (e.g. patent-citation measurement) with no people, organizations, or society angle.
